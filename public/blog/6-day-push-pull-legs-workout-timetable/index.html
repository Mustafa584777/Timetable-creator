1. Introduction: Why the 6-Day Push Pull Legs (PPL) Split Is the Gold Standard for Muscle Growth

The 6-day PPL split is the gold standard because every major muscle group is hit twice per week with enough rest to grow. I used to train only 3 days a week and my gains were slow. After switching to 6 days with smart recovery, I added 8–10 kg to my bench press and deadlift in just 12 weeks. This timetable gives you the highest training frequency possible while protecting your body.

2. How the 6-Day PPL Split Works: Anatomy, Frequency & Recovery Science

Muscle Group Breakdown: Push (Chest, Shoulders, Triceps) vs. Pull (Back, Rear Delts, Biceps) vs. Legs

  • Push Day: Chest, front and side shoulders, triceps
  • Pull Day: Back, rear shoulders, biceps
  • Legs Day: Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, core

This balanced split ensures you train every muscle twice a week without overtraining any one group.

The 48-to-72-Hour Recovery Window: Hitting Every Muscle Twice a Week Safely

Every muscle group gets trained with at least 48 hours between sessions and up to 72 hours between the two sessions per week. This is the perfect frequency for both beginners and intermediates.

Who Should Run a 6-Day PPL Routine? (Beginner vs. Intermediate Readiness Check)

I only recommend 6-day PPL for people who have been training consistently for at least 3–6 months. Beginners should start with a 7-day weekly gym workout plan for beginners (4–5 lifting days) and then add a rest day or switch to 3-day PPL. If you are completely new, this plan will still work but you must reduce volume in the first 4 weeks.

3. Content Gap: Choosing Your Weekly PPL Timetable (Fixed vs. Rolling Schedules)

Option 1: The Fixed Monday–Saturday Timetable (6-On / 1-Off Synchronous Plan)

I use this version when I want maximum consistency. Lift on Monday–Saturday and rest on Sunday. This is the simplest and most popular option.

Option 2: The Rolling 3-On / 1-Off Timetable (Best for Joint & CNS Recovery)

This rolling version (e.g., Mon–Wed–Fri, then next week Tue–Thu–Sat) gives your joints and nervous system more recovery. I recommend this for the first 12 weeks if you have any joint history or train at night.

Sequence Hack: Why "Pull / Push / Legs" Beats "Push / Pull / Legs" for Lower-Back Recovery

I always do Pull first, then Push, then Legs. This order protects my lower back because heavy deadlifts and squats come at the end of the week when fatigue is highest.

4. The Complete 6-Day Push Pull Legs Workout Timetable (Strength & Hypertrophy)

Day 1 – Push A (Heavy Strength Focus: Chest, Anterior Delts & Triceps)

  • Bench Press – 4 sets of 5–6 (heavy)
  • Overhead Press – 4 sets of 6–8
  • Incline Dumbbell Press – 3 sets of 8–10
  • Tricep Pushdowns – 3 sets of 10–12
  • Dips or Close-Grip Bench – 3 sets to near failure

Day 2 – Pull A (Vertical Pull & Thickness Focus: Lats, Traps, Rear Delts & Biceps)

  • Pull-Ups or Lat Pulldowns – 4 sets of 6–8
  • Bent-Over Rows – 4 sets of 8–10
  • Seated Cable Rows – 3 sets of 10–12
  • Face Pulls – 3 sets of 15–20
  • Bicep Curls – 3 sets of 10–12

Day 3 – Legs A (Quad-Dominant Compound Focus: Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes & Calves)

  • Squats or Leg Press – 4 sets of 6–8
  • Romanian Deadlifts – 4 sets of 8–10
  • Bulgarian Split Squats – 3 sets per leg
  • Leg Extensions – 3 sets of 12–15
  • Seated Calf Raises – 4 sets of 12–15

Day 4 – Push B (Upper Chest & Lateral Delt Hypertrophy Focus)

  • Incline Bench Press – 4 sets of 8–10
  • Arnold Press or Lateral Raises – 4 sets of 10–12
  • Cable Crossovers – 3 sets of 12–15
  • Tricep Dips or Overhead Extension – 3 sets of 10–12
  • Lateral Raises – 3 sets of 15–20

Day 5 – Pull B (Horizontal Row & Arm Isolation Focus)

  • Weighted Pull-Ups or Bent-Over Rows – 4 sets of 6–8
  • Single-Arm Dumbbell Rows – 4 sets of 8–10 per side
  • Shrugs or Upright Rows – 3 sets of 10–12
  • Hammer Curls – 3 sets of 10–12
  • Concentration Curls – 3 sets of 12–15

Day 6 – Legs B (Posterior Chain & Hinge Focus: Hamstrings, Glutes & Core)

  • Deadlifts or Good Mornings – 4 sets of 6–8
  • Hip Thrusts – 4 sets of 8–10
  • Lying Leg Curls – 3 sets of 10–12
  • Standing Calf Raises – 4 sets of 12–15
  • Hanging Leg Raises – 3 sets of 12–15

Day 7 – Dedicated Rest & Recovery Day

Full rest. No workouts, no intense cardio. Focus on sleep, hydration, and eating.

5. Strategic Gap: The Complete PPL Rest Day & Deload Guide

Complete Rest vs. Active Recovery: What to Actually Do on Day 7

On Sunday I do complete rest with no exercise. Active recovery (light walking, stretching, or 10-minute cardio) is only for the Thursday day.

Rest Day Nutrition & Protein Rules: Should You Lower Calories When Not Lifting?

Eat at maintenance or slight surplus. Protein stays at 1.6–2.2g per kg body weight. No need to eat in deficit on rest days.

Warning Signs You Need an Extra Unplanned Rest Day (CNS Fatigue Checklist)

  • Joint pain that feels sharp
  • Overuse soreness that doesn’t improve after 48 hours
  • Extreme fatigue that makes you miss 2+ consecutive workouts
  • Sleep quality drops sharply

If any of these happen, take the full rest day.

The 6-to-8-Week Deload Protocol: How to Reset Fatigue Without Losing Gains

Every 6–8 weeks I do a 4-day deload (50% volume, 30% intensity) with the same exercises. This resets CNS fatigue and keeps long-term progress intact.

6. Programming Rules: Sets, Reps, Rest Intervals & Progressive Overload

Weekly Volume Caps: Avoiding "Junk Volume" Across 6 Training Days

Total weekly sets per muscle group should stay under 20–25 sets for intermediate lifters. I cap chest at 20 sets, back at 22 sets, and legs at 24 sets.

Optimal Rest Times Between Heavy Compounds (2–3 Mins) vs. Isolations (60–90 Secs)

Heavy compounds (squat, bench, deadlift) get 2–3 minutes. Isolation moves (rows, curls) get 60–90 seconds. This keeps workouts 60–75 minutes long.

Double Progression & The 1–2 Reps in Reserve (RIR) Rule

I use double progression: add weight when I hit the top of the rep range, then add reps when I hit the bottom. I stop every set at 1–2 reps in reserve.

7. Customizing the 6-Day PPL Timetable for Your Lifestyle

Time-Crunched 45-Minute PPL Sessions (Supersets & Drop Sets)

I use supersets (push-pull or quad-ham) and drop sets on isolation exercises to finish workouts in under 45 minutes while maintaining volume.

Home & Dumbbell-Only Push Pull Legs Substitutions

At home I replace heavy squats with goblet squats, bench press with floor press, and pull-ups with inverted rows. The structure stays exactly the same.

How to Adjust the Timetable When You Miss a Workout Day

If I miss one day, I simply shift the next workout forward by one day and repeat the missed workout on the next available slot. This keeps the frequency perfect.

8. Common 6-Day PPL Mistakes That Stall Progress (And How to Fix Them)

Training to Absolute Failure on Every Set Early in the Week

I never train to failure in the first two PPL cycles. Save failure for the final set of every exercise.

Skipping Leg Day B or Neglecting Rear Delts and Calves

Leg Day B always includes deadlifts and good mornings. Rear delts and calves get hit twice a week no matter what.

Doing High-Intensity Cardio Right Before Heavy Leg Sessions

I never do intense cardio before legs. Light walking is fine, but never HIIT on training days.
